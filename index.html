Scope

Four things I check, in every tenant.

You choose the whole audit or a single area. Either way you get findings ranked by what they cost you, not by what's easiest to fix.

CRM health

Whether Dynamics 365 still matches how your team actually sells and serves — or how it did four consultants ago.

  • Security roles and record ownership
  • Customizations, plugins, unmanaged solutions
  • Workflows and Power Automate flows still running
  • Adoption: who logs in, who works around it

Security & Entra ID

Who can reach what, and what happens the day an account is compromised.

  • Privileged accounts and standing admin rights
  • MFA and conditional access coverage
  • Guest access, stale accounts, orphaned mailboxes
  • App registrations and API permissions

Licenses

Most tenants pay for seats and tiers nobody needs. This part of the audit usually pays for the rest of it.

  • Assigned vs. actually used, per user
  • Overlapping plans and duplicate add-ons
  • Right-sizing before your next renewal
  • A number you can take to procurement

Data & integrations

Reports are only as good as what feeds them. This is where trust in the CRM is usually lost.

  • Duplicates, dead records, missing owners
  • Integrations that fail silently
  • Backup, retention and export reality
  • What your dashboards actually measure

How it works

Three steps, about two weeks.

Step 01

Read-only access

A short call to agree on scope, then read-only access to the tenant. I change nothing without asking, and I sign your NDA before I see anything.

Step 02

The audit

I go through configuration, permissions, licenses and data, and talk to two or three people who use the system daily. Most of it happens without your team in the room.

Step 03

Report and walkthrough

A written report — every finding with its impact, a fix, and who should own it — plus a call to walk your team through it. Your IT partner can implement the fixes; so can I.
